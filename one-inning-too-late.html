Infinite Cincinnati

One Inning Too Late

1972 World Series @ Wikipedia

1972 World Series Game 4 @ baseball-reference.com

Cincinnati scored in the 9th inning in 8 of 35 timelines.

In 5 of those 8 games, the 9th-inning runs tied the game or gave Cincinnati the lead. Cincinnati won all 5.

In the other 3 games, the 9th-inning runs fell short. Cincinnati scored 2, 2, and 4 runs in the 9th inning and lost all three times.

The Big Red Machine was a team that arrived late. Sometimes just in time. Sometimes one inning too late.

Timeline 28: Four Runs and It Didn't Matter (L, OAK 5-4 CIN)

The line score:

CIN: 0 0 0   0 0 0   0 0 4 - 4

OAK: 2 0 0   0 3 0   0 0 0 - 5

For eight innings, Cincinnati did not score. Not a single run. Oakland scored 2 in the 1st and 3 in the 5th. After eight innings, the score was 0-5. Cincinnati had been shut out for twenty-four consecutive half-innings.

Then the 9th inning happened.

Johnny Bench hit a grand slam. Four runs. Four RBI on one swing. The score went from 0-5 to 4-5.

That is the most runs Cincinnati scored in any single inning across 35 timelines. The biggest inning they ever had. And it happened while they were trailing by five runs in the bottom of the 9th.

It was not enough.

Cincinnati needed five. They got four. One run short. One swing short. One inning too late.

If that grand slam had happened in the 8th inning instead of the 9th, there would have been another inning to score one more run. If it had happened in the 7th, there would have been two more innings. But it happened in the 9th, because Cincinnati's offense could not find the 9th inning until the 9th inning.

Timeline 18: Down Five, Won Anyway (W, CIN 6-5 OAK)

The line score:

CIN: 0 0 0   3 0 0   0 0 3 - 6

OAK: 4 0 1   0 0 0   0 0 0 - 5

Oakland scored 4 runs in the 1st inning. They added another in the 3rd. After three innings, the score was 0-5. Cincinnati was being demolished.

Then Cincinnati scored 3 in the 4th. The score became 3-5. And then nothing. The 5th was scoreless. The 6th. The 7th. The 8th. Four innings of silence while Cincinnati trailed by two.

Top of the 9th. Cincinnati scored 3 runs. CIN 6, OAK 5. Game over.

This game is Timeline 28's twin. Same deficit. Same 0-5 hole. Same team clawing back. The difference is that Timeline 18's Cincinnati arrived in two installments - 3 in the 4th, 3 in the 9th - instead of one. They spread the comeback across two innings instead of concentrating it in one.

And that was enough. Three in the 4th cut the deficit to two. Three in the 9th erased it. The same number of runs - 6 - that would have won Timeline 28 if Cincinnati had found one more.

Oakland scored all 5 of their runs in the first 3 innings and then went silent for 6 consecutive innings. Cincinnati scored all 6 of their runs in two bursts and won. Oakland was a sprinter. Cincinnati was a closer. The closer won.

Timeline 17: The Greatest Comeback, One More Time (W, CIN 7-6 OAK, 10 inn)

The line score:

CIN: 0 0 0   0 0 0   0 4 2   1 - 7

OAK: 0 0 0   0 0 0   6 0 0   0 - 6

This game appears on the Cincinnati in the Fifteenth page. It is the greatest comeback in 35 timelines. But it belongs here too, because it is the purest expression of Cincinnati's late-arriving offense.

Twelve consecutive half-innings of scoreless baseball. Then Oakland scored 6 in the bottom of the 7th. The score was 0-6 with 9 outs remaining.

Cincinnati scored 4 in the 8th. Then 2 in the 9th. Then 1 in the 10th.

The comeback arrived in declining increments - 4, 2, 1 - like a heartbeat slowing toward the finish. Each burst was exactly enough to keep the game alive, and then to win it. But the first burst did not arrive until the 8th inning. For seven innings, Cincinnati produced nothing. The Machine was silent. And then, with extinction nine outs away, it turned on.

The Pattern

Cincinnati scored in the 9th inning in 8 of 35 games. They scored a total of 16 runs in those 8 ninth innings.

Five of those games, they won. Three of those games, they lost.

The three losses: they scored 2, 2, and 4 in the 9th. Eight total runs in losing 9th innings. Eight runs that arrived too late to change the outcome. Eight runs that proved Cincinnati could hit - just not soon enough.

The five wins: they scored 1, 1, 1, 2, and 3 in the 9th. Eight total runs in winning 9th innings. The same number. The same eight runs. But these eight arrived on time.

Oakland scored in the 9th inning in 5 games. All 5 were walk-offs. All 5 were single runs. Oakland did not need a 4-run 9th inning. Oakland did not need a comeback. Oakland scored 1 run in the 9th and won, five times. Clean. Surgical. Final.

Cincinnati needed eruptions. Oakland needed a single.

The Big Red Machine was not built for surgical strikes. It was built for demolition. And in a World Series of one-run games, demolition arrives one inning too late.
